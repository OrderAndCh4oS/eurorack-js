# Fold Percussion (`fold-perc`)

Fold Percussion is a monophonic, triggered `voice` inspired by Noise Engineering’s 10 HP Basimilus Iteritas **Alter**. It produces tuned kicks, noisy drum strikes and inharmonic metallic hits from six pitched oscillators plus a transient noise source. Both the oscillator interactions and the timbre of a sounding hit respond to CV. This is an independent synthesis design. The original manufacturer discontinued the Alter and identifies Alia as its successor. [Noise Engineering, legacy product page](https://noiseengineering.us/products/basimilus-iteritas-alter/)

## Evidence and scope

The original English manual gives 10 HP and 1.5 inches (38.1 mm) depth. Using ±12 V alone it draws 150 mA on +12 V and 5 mA on −12 V. With the optional +5 V supply the manual lists 80 mA, 5 mA and 90 mA on +12 V, −12 V and +5 V respectively. The current translated page lists different dimensions and power. The original Alter manual identifies the specific 10 HP instrument used here. [Original Noise Engineering manual, p. 1](https://d1aeri3ty3izns.cloudfront.net/media/39/390618/download_390618.pdf)

The legacy manual shows independently decaying partials mixed before an attack contour, a level-compensated reflection folder, and a second envelope that restores the transient and tail after folding. Skin sums the oscillators. Liquid adds a common downward pitch sweep. In the Metal diagram, the output of an enveloped operator linearly modulates the frequency of its neighbor. Spread moves oscillator intervals from a harmonic series towards a prime series. The text identifies a separate noise generator. [Noise Engineering, original Alter manual, c. 2016–17, pp. 2–7](https://d1aeri3ty3izns.cloudfront.net/media/39/390618/download_390618.pdf) · [Manufacturer’s original synthesis drawing](https://noiseengineering.us/static/710eaf7b3bff8280a4ac1658139ff080/1cfc2/synthesis.png)

The Alter used fixed-point math, pitch-dependent sampling, and a 16-bit DAC. Its changing sample rate placed some aliases on harmonics of the played note. In the browser the AudioWorklet runs at the host’s fixed rate. The project chooses a bounded FM depth and bandwidth-limited waveforms so high pitches stay useful. Exact FM gains, oscillator phases, envelope constants, blend laws, knob tuning endpoints and switch-voltage thresholds are design choices here. The manufacturer’s later desktop plugin describes two three-operator PM stacks. The Alter drawing is the reference for this voice. [Alter design notes, pp. 4–7](https://d1aeri3ty3izns.cloudfront.net/media/39/390618/download_390618.pdf) · [Noise Engineering, Basimilus Iteritas software instrument manual](https://noiseengineering.us/manuals/basimilus-iteritas/)

The original English manual puts the analog-style pop at noon. A German sentence on the current Alter manual page also places it at the left stop, despite describing noise in the left half. The English manual and player reports agree on noon. The German manual specifies 0–5 V control inputs, 0–8 V pitch, and a rising trigger near 3 V. ModularGrid’s 2016–17 discussion corroborates the 10 HP size and added switch CVs, but reverses the direction of the knob/CV change. The Alter’s knobs add offsets. ## Panel and voltage contract

Use the rack name **FOLD PERC**, 16 HP, `module-color-seven`. A wider virtual panel accommodates seven compact knobs, two named three-position selectors, the Hit action and eleven jacks. Group the controls visually as time (Attack, Decay), tone (Pitch, Morph, Harmonic, Spread, Fold) and mode/register. Prefer the declarative renderer. Keep `Skin`, `Liquid`, `Metal` and `Bass`, `Alto`, `Treble` readable at normal rack zoom. `hit` is a declarative `trigger` action, default 0. `active` is a strike LED held for about 50 ms in real time. The manual identifies a manual Hit button and gives no LED timing.

| Parameter | Default | Control law and effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pitch` | 0.25 | 0–1 sweeps 27.5–440 Hz in Bass, exponentially. At 0.25 the unpatched fundamental is 55 Hz. |
| `attack` | 0.5 | 0–0.5 blends a short decaying noise transient into the fast strike. Noise reaches zero at noon, giving the pop. Above noon progressively soften the onset to about 100 ms at maximum. |
| `decay` | 0.45 | 0–1 sets the exponential body duration, roughly 60 ms–2.4 s to −60 dB for a single dry sine. Upper partials have their own shorter decays. |
| `morph` | 0 | Smoothly interpolate all operators through sine, triangle, saw and square at 0, ⅓, ⅔ and 1. |
| `harmonic` | 0.35 | Zero isolates the fundamental. Before 0.25 the second partial fades in. The remaining range introduces short higher partials, extends their tails and increases their level. |
| `spread` | 0 | Move only the upper five frequency ratios. Use `[1,2,3,4,5,6]` at zero and `[1,3,5,7,11,13]` at maximum. Intermediate ratios include non-integers. The choice of odd primes and interpolation is ours. |
| `fold` | 0.15 | At zero the folder is transparent. Through 0.75 reduce the reflection threshold with level compensation. Above 0.75 add a short bipolar pulse at extrema for buzz. The pulse contribution grows continuously from zero. |
| `mode` | 0 | Positions 0 Skin, 1 Liquid, 2 Metal. |
| `range` | 0 | Positions 0 Bass, 1 Alto, 2 Treble add 0, 2 and 4 octaves respectively. |

| Port | Signal and declared voltage | Normal and behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pitchCV` (V/Oct) | CV, 0–8 V | 0 V. Each added volt doubles fundamental frequency within the audio band. Add to the panel’s tuning in log-frequency space. |
| `attackCV`, `decayCV`, `morphCV`, `harmonicCV`, `spreadCV`, `foldCV` | CV, 0–5 V | 0 V. Clamp the supplied voltage to the declared range and add voltage / 5 to its matching knob, then clamp the result to 0–1. A +2.5 V source supplies half a control range. |
| `modeCV` (S/L/M), `rangeCV` (B/A/T) | CV, 0–5 V | 0 V. The digital switch-CV mapping is an assumption. Add 0, 1 or 2 positions to the panel selection in three equal bands with boundaries at 5/3 and 10/3 V, saturating at position 2. From position 0, 0, 2.5 and 5 V select all three states. |
| `trigger` (Trig) | Trigger, 0–10 V | 0 V. Fire on crossing from below 3 V to ≥3 V. A typical 5–10 ms rack pulse strikes once. A held high signal rings out, with no sustain. |
| `out` | Mono audio, −5 to +5 V | Silent until the first trigger or manual Hit. A VCA or external envelope is optional. |

All ten inputs have stable, zero-normaled buffers. Sample incoming CV through the block, including changes during tails. Handle jack and button edges independently so holding one still permits strikes from the other. Coincident edges give one hit. A retrigger replaces the current hit using a short transition to limit arbitrary voltage steps. Reset silences the voice immediately, restores deterministic oscillator and noise state, clears edge memory, restores buffers in place and extinguishes the LED. No input acts as an audio excitation or reset jack.

## DSP plan and trade-offs

1. Allocate the six phases, envelopes and other fixed-size state in `createDSP()`. Start a short monophonic transient on a rising edge and update the operator amplitudes independently. Use a locally seeded generator for the burst below noon. Reset must reproduce the same first noisy hit. A fast clean onset at noon must be audible even with a lone sine and no fold.
2. Compute the fundamental from panel pitch, pitch CV and register. In Liquid multiply every frequency by the same exponentially falling pitch ratio, starting roughly two octaves up and converging to the Skin pitch. In Metal let each higher operator’s enveloped output modulate the next lower frequency and mix the audible taps. Weight modulation with Harmonic so isolating the fundamental still works. Keep the FM depth finite and taper upper operators approaching Nyquist. Sample-rate-safe limits at extreme pitch are part of the adaptation.
3. Blend phase-aligned waveforms. Reuse `wrapPhase()` and `polyBlep()` for discontinuous waves or band-limited equivalent tables. Mix weighted operators and the Attack noise, apply the onset contour, then fold the bipolar sum. Keep the average output centered by using a symmetric folder and opposite pulse polarities for maxima/minima. Reapply a body contour after folding to preserve the decay. Bound `out` at ±5 V without relying on the output module to clip it.
4. Avoid raw high-pitch saw/square discontinuities and unlimited nonlinear drive. First-order antiderivative antialiasing for piecewise reflection, or short fixed oversampling with low-pass decimation, reduces nonlinear aliases. A bounded analytic reflection replaces a variable-length loop through extra stages. At extreme settings residual high-frequency sidebands are expected. A wavefolder with a pure sine transfer would be simpler but changes the Alter’s repeating reflection shape. Reuse this project’s oscillator utilities, envelope/reset approach from Kick, and anti-aliasing guidance from Fold. ## Test targets

- Verify factory defaults, three labels on each selector, seven knobs, Hit, LED, every voltage declaration and all buffer identities. Prefill output with NaNs and check every sample is overwritten. With no strike the entire output must be zero.
- Schedule a jack edge inside a block. At 2.99 V the signal stays silent. At 3.00 V it starts on that edge, finishes under a held gate and retriggers after one low sample. Check manual hits, including a press under a held jack, two successive presses and simultaneous edges. Distinct hits must restart the decay. Test a 5 ms pulse and a one-sample pulse.
- In Skin, Harmonic/Morph/Fold at zero and Attack at noon, estimate dry 55 Hz and ratios 2, 4 and 16 for +1 V, Alto and Treble respectively within 2%. Use long decay and skip the initial transient. At full Harmonic locate six modal peaks near the chosen spread endpoints. At minimum Harmonic the second through sixth peaks must each be at least 25 dB below the fundamental.
- In Liquid, compare early and late zero-crossing periods with Skin. Early frequency must exceed the tail by at least 50%, and settle to within 5% of Skin. At substantial Harmonic and fractional Spread, check that Metal adds sidebands absent from Skin under otherwise identical settings. Check a lower Spread, Harmonic-above-noon case audibly for a bell-like pitched tail.
- Compare short/long Decay and first 10 ms RMS at noon/full Attack. At full Attack the early RMS must be lower than noon. At zero Attack the transient contains more high-band energy than at noon. At minimum decay the tail falls below 1% of peak by 200 ms, while maximum decay remains above 1% at 500 ms. Check post-fold decay still drops when Fold is maximum.
- Compare the four Morph landmarks using a dry fundamental. Triangle and square emphasize odd harmonics, saw includes even harmonics, and sine has the weakest overtones. Changes just either side of ⅓ and ⅔ approach the same output. Compare Fold 0, 0.5, 0.75 and 1 with a single sine, checking added harmonics and the top-quarter contribution. Spread must leave the fundamental fixed and move upper spectral peaks. Record a narrowband high-pitch or reference-render measurement when tuning the alias controls.
- For each of the six timbre jacks separately, compare a 2.5 V offset to +0.5 on its matching knob with all other settings fixed. Compare both selector jacks at 0, 2.5 and 5 V to equivalent panel positions and check saturation from the middle position. Include CV changes away from sample zero. Verify that reset reproduces the first hit sample for sample, including noise. Compare the same event stream across block sizes and check bounded, finite output, LED decay and stress settings across all six audit configurations.

## DSP Audit (2026-11-25)

The implementation uses phase-aligned PolyBLEP saw and square shapes, reduces discontinuous-wave content at high increments, and tapers upper operators between 0.32 and 0.46 times the sample rate. Metal runs the frequency-modulation chain from operator six down to one. A pair of exponential body envelopes straddles the piecewise-linear reflection folder. Its first antiderivative integrates both the recurring reflections and the narrow bipolar contribution at the extrema. A replacement hit crossfades from the last sample over 0.8 ms.

- All six rate/block-size audit combinations (44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, 128 and 512 frames) passed 22 scenarios apiece. Peak magnitude ranged from 4.947 to 4.982 V with stable buffers and finite in-range output.
- At 48 kHz the dry Skin fixture tracked 55 Hz, doubled under +1 V and rose to 220/880 Hz in Alto/Treble. Full Harmonic at a 110 Hz fundamental produced six resolved peaks at both Spread endpoints. On the fractional-Spread fixture the 165 Hz difference sideband measured 0.064 V in Metal and below 0.00005 V in Skin.
- For a 5.6 kHz square strike, the fifth harmonic reflected to 20 kHz measured −24.7 dB relative to the fundamental, against −13.9 dB for an equivalent naive square. With a sine strike and Fold at halfway, the 20 kHz component measured 1.17 V after antiderivative antialiasing versus 2.31 V with direct reflection. These measurements used 48 kHz, maximum decay, and a Hann window on samples 1200–14399.
- The focused tests also exercise independent jack/button edges, the 3 V threshold, 50 ms LED hold, deterministic noisy reset, equivalent timbre offsets and the selector boundaries at the nearest representable input voltages. [Focused DSP tests](../../tests/dsp/fold-perc.test.js)
