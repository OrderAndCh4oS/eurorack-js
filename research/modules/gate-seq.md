# Gate Seq

Four editable gate tracks share an external clock. A player can keep a kick on a 16-step cycle, run a seven-step hat against it, and play a fill across all four tracks without losing their place. This is a digital sequencer inspired by Intellijel Steppy 3U. Its independent track multipliers extend the reference instrument. [Steppy](https://intellijel.com/shop/eurorack/steppy/) · [Steppy 3U manual, firmware 1.2](https://intellijel.com/downloads/manuals/steppy_manual_1.2_2020.10.26.pdf)

## DSP, persistence and assumptions

1. **Transport.** Maintain integer sample time across blocks and four independent track counters. The first external rising edge visits step 1 on every track. ÷N then advances after N more edges. Once a second external edge measures a period `P`, ×M schedules the M−1 interior advances at `round(kP/M)` from the latest edge. Before measuring a period, issue one step on the first edge and wait for the next real clock before multiplying. Expire predictions by the end of one input period, replace any remaining multiplied advances on an early real edge, and prioritize a real edge over a coincident prediction. A stopped clock cannot replay the same prediction indefinitely. Sample-domain scheduling follows [DIV](../../src/js/modules/div/index.js), [PROB SEQ](../../src/js/modules/prob-seq/index.js) and the [W3C Web Audio rendering model](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/#rendering-loop).
2. **Reset.** RST clears gates, tie state, predictions, divider phase, fill cursor and normal playheads immediately. A coincident rising CLK then starts step 1 exactly once. RST alone leaves step 1 armed for the next clock. Keep the last measured period on a jack reset for rhythmic continuity, while invalidating the last edge timestamp so a pause across reset cannot corrupt that estimate. A full DSP reset also clears clock history, timing estimates, random state, telemetry and buffers, preserving patch parameters and stable array identities. This sample priority is an explicit rack choice. Steppy's firmware accommodates a reset arriving shortly *after* a clock and offers RUN mode. [Manual, firmware history](https://intellijel.com/downloads/manuals/steppy_manual_1.2_2020.10.26.pdf#page=38)
3. **Step visit and timing.** Advance normal position even on rests, muted tracks or rejected hits. Rotation maps the normal position back into its active-length ring, so a +1 rotation sends a hit from position 1 to 2. On fill engagement start each track at the lower endpoint of the span on its next due step and wrap inclusively. Its parallel normal counter continues. On release resume the next normal cell. Gate enable and ratchet count always travel together. Read out-of-length fill cells from the full 64-cell backing pattern. Boundaries between active-length and extended fill positions should have one documented rotation rule, for example rotate the active cycle and read extended cells at their explicit addresses. Mute immediately closes active gates and cancels pending repeats, while unmute waits for a new enabled step. [Steppy live loop](https://intellijel.com/downloads/manuals/steppy_manual_1.2_2020.10.26.pdf#page=19)
4. **Gate generation.** For nominal track step period T, added delay is `round(T × (delay/100 + (offbeat ? 2 × swing/100 − 1 : 0)))`. Alternate swing on successive track ticks independently of pattern rotation and odd-length wraps. Clip the summed delay to leave at least one sample in the current step. A single gate uses the shorter of its requested width and the remaining step window, leaving at least one low sample before a separate hit where physically possible. A 100% gate can sustain into a consecutive enabled single hit with zero intervening rest or onset gap. It expires at its predicted boundary even if CLK stops. A ratcheted hit schedules evenly spaced onsets across the remaining window and uses short pulses (nominally one twenty-fourth of T), capped by the spacing with at least one low sample. Coalesce subdivisions closer than two samples and cancel any unfinished step at its next actual track tick. Before the first measured interval, use a 125 ms length estimate for the first step window, leaving multiplication disabled until lock. This estimate generates no clocks. [Manual, pp. 21, 23, 30–32](https://intellijel.com/downloads/manuals/steppy_manual_1.2_2020.10.26.pdf#page=22) · [Sound engineering review](../sound-engineering-review.md)
5. **Chance and state cost.** Use a deterministic, independently seeded generator per track. Decide once per eligible cell so a successful ratchet plays all repeats. Zero and 100 percent are exact. Allocate fixed state in `createDSP()` and schedule with integer offsets. Four tracks, up to 12 scheduled hits each, require constant memory. Cache decoded values on working-pattern identity changes. Keep the preset bank off the per-sample path. Quarter-cycle UI updates or JavaScript wall-clock timers would move edges when the tab stalls. [Probability Sequencer](../../src/js/modules/prob-seq/index.js) · [Rack audio thread](../../docs/architecture.md#module-instances)
6. **Patch state.** Declare finite structured `pattern` and `presets` in `ui.state`. Keep the pattern bounded to four track records with 64 gate/ratchet cells each. Each slot holds an independent copy or an empty sentinel. A compact numeric cell representation is suitable when separate enabled and ratchet values survive disabling a cell. Default to four blank editable tracks and eight empty memories. Saving replaces only the bank. Loading replaces only the working pattern and keeps underlying transport phase modulo the new lengths. Recreate runtime cursors when loading a rack patch. Persist fill endpoint settings and declare Fill as a momentary action that loads released. Sanitize missing, malformed or excessive structured values to bounded finite defaults. Mirror synchronization and patch export must work before starting audio. Bounded telemetry can consist of a four-element array of currently addressed steps and the six named LEDs. No history, audio buffers or presets are needed in display telemetry. [Patch v3 and custom telemetry](../../docs/architecture.md#custom-renderer-telemetry)

## DSP Audit (2026-11-25)

The 16 HP rack panel exposes Steps, Settings and Memory views and keeps the four mutes, fill endpoints, momentary Fill and six jacks visible in every view. In patch data, `pattern` holds four track records. Each of 64 signed cell values stores the hit count by magnitude and gate enable by sign. A disabled ordinary step is −1. The eight entries of `presets` are independent whole patterns or null. Gate Seq opts out of parameter echo in display telemetry, so the worklet sends only six LEDs and four addressed positions. The host remains the authority for edits.

Focused tests confirmed ÷64, ×4 and triplet clock ratios, step 64 wrap, three-hit ratchets at frames 120/160/200, 25% gates exactly 30 frames wide with a 120-frame clock, deterministic independent probability and identical outputs at block sizes 1 and 128. Multiplied ties stay high across differently rounded subdivisions and end at the predicted boundary if the clock stops. A simultaneous clock/reset during an active gate visits step 1 and reserves one low sample before retriggering.

Renderer and host tests saved and recalled all eight slots, round-tripped the pattern with audio off, edited track D step 64, and released Fill on keyboard, pointer cancellation, focus loss and teardown. A production worklet snapshot captured before subsequent gate and preset edits left both newer values intact when delivered to the host.

The strict matrix also passed with a programmed stress pattern in addition to all 130 control scenarios. At 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz with 128- and 512-sample blocks, each of A–D reached 10 V, remained inside its declared range and retained its buffer identity. The four active cells used 1, 3, 6 and 12 hits respectively.
