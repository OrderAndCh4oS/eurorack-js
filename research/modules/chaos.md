# Chaos — Continuous Chaotic CV Generator

## Research Status

- Researched: 2026-07-31.
- Queue target: `chaos`, risk `Medium`, category `modulation`.
- Proposed gate: **spec-ready**. The coordinator owns the queue transition; this
  research branch deliberately does not edit `research/module-queue.md`.
- Scope: an inspired software adaptation of a continuous Lorenz flow, informed
  by Nonlinearcircuits Sloth and Joranalogue Orbit 3 interaction patterns. It is
  not a component-level emulation of either circuit.
- Implementation, tests, registration, graph revision changes, and the factory
  patch are explicitly out of scope for this research branch.

## Purpose and Distinct Capability

`chaos` supplies three continuously evolving, correlated voltages generated by
one autonomous nonlinear state. The path is deterministic and locally smooth,
but at its default parameters it is aperiodic and sensitive to small changes.
This is the useful middle ground described by the Orbit 3 manual: more
repeatable and structurally related than independent randomness, without an
LFO's fixed period.

The module remains distinct from every nearby core module:

| Existing module | Existing behavior | What `chaos` adds |
|---|---|---|
| `rnd` | Seeded PCG draws, sample-and-hold, slew, and probabilistic gates | No PRNG or draws: X/Y/Z are simultaneous coordinates of one continuous deterministic trajectory. |
| `lfo`, `quad-lfo`, `ochd` | Periodic phase accumulators, including related phases and free-running rates | No phase cycle or fixed period: lobe residence and crossings vary while the axes remain dynamically correlated. |
| `turing` | Clocked finite shift-register loops with probabilistic bit mutation | No clock, steps, register, finite loop length, or lock control. |
| `sh` | Holds a sampled input on trigger | No sampling or held steps; all three CV outputs evolve every sample. |

This boundary is normative. Adding a clocked logistic-map mode would weaken it:
Robert May's classic account treats the logistic family as a first-order
**difference** equation, so a musical panel would need an update clock and
probably slew. That would reproduce much of `rnd`/`sh` rather than add a new
continuous modulation behavior. The first implementation therefore uses one
continuous Lorenz system only.

### Deliberate non-goals

- No random-number generator, probability control, seed knob, or saved mutable
  continuation state.
- No clocked sequence, quantization, sample-and-hold, or loop/lock mode.
- No audio-rate/VCO range or pitch tracking. Orbit 3 demonstrates that chaos
  can be useful at audio rates, but this queue item is specifically a modulation
  source; limiting the rate keeps the CPU and anti-aliasing scope bounded.
- No claim to reproduce Sloth's op-amp circuit, Orbit 3's modified double-scroll
  core, component tolerances, or analogue noise.
- No custom renderer or phase-space display. The factory patch uses the existing
  Scope in X-Y mode instead.

## Evidence Summary and Observed Behavior

### Hardware behavior carried into the adaptation

- Nonlinearcircuits describes the single Sloth as a source of slow,
  never-repeating changes. Its panel control changes the balance of the chaotic
  orbit rather than acting as a conventional frequency knob. The Triple Sloth
  extends this idea to several rates and related stage outputs.
- The current Triple Sloth guide gives approximate orbit times of 15–30 seconds,
  60–90 seconds, and 30–40 minutes. It also warns that injected CV can produce
  periodic windows or voltage jumps. Those are useful reminders that a chaos
  control does not monotonically mean “more randomness.”
- The single-Sloth guide describes lobe dwell explicitly: a trajectory can stay
  in one well for several revolutions, and its bipolar LED changes colour when
  the observed output crosses 0 V. This motivates a lobe-state gate and
  sign-derived LEDs rather than a regular clock output.
- Joranalogue describes Orbit 3 as deterministic rather than random, with three
  coordinates around two equilibrium points. Its rate, attractor parameters,
  reset, correlated outputs, X-Y oscilloscope use, and equilibrium-point gate
  establish a strong Eurorack interaction precedent for the proposed panel.
- Orbit 3's manual says parameter combinations can yield stable oscillation,
  bistability, or full chaos. The proposed Character control intentionally
  includes an ordered edge; a temporary periodic or settled region is not a DSP
  failure if outputs remain finite and reset can restart the documented state.

### Independent observations

- Richard Brewster's hands-on single-Sloth report observed about 20 seconds for
  Lethargy and about a minute for Apathy, a sign-changing bipolar LED, a roughly
  ±3 V Small output, and a Large output that moves differently rather than merely
  being a scaled copy.
- Sound + Voltage's “Sloths 201” demonstration focuses on 11 slow, correlated
  outputs evolving generative music over minutes and hours. Its earlier
  Sloth/Akemie's Castle patch visibly follows the slow trajectory on a scope
  while one output changes an FM multiplier.
- Waveform Magazine's Orbit 3 review reports that Tame/Wild changes can be much
  clearer on an X-Y display than by ear, that the module retains a recognizable
  character while remaining hard to predict, and that using several related
  axes together is more revealing than treating one output as a “random LFO.”
- These observations support correlated X/Y/Z outputs and a scope-oriented
  factory patch. They do not establish exact transfer functions and are not used
  as electrical specifications.

### Parameters

| Parameter | UI | Range/default | Normative behavior |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `rate` | Knob, `Rate` | `0..1`, default `0.5` | Exponential model-time speed before CV: `0.02 * 1000^rate` model units per real second, giving `0.02..20`. This changes traversal speed without changing the Lorenz geometry. |
| `character` | Knob, `Character` | `0..1`, default `1/3` | Sets Lorenz `rho = 24 + 12 * effectiveCharacter`; default is the classic `rho = 28`. Lower settings approach ordered/stable behavior; the default and much of the range produce the intended double-lobed chaotic trajectory. |
| `depth` | Knob, `Depth` | `0..1`, default `1` | Linear post-conditioner gain for X/Y/Z only. It does not alter state evolution or the Lobe output. |

There are no switches, buttons, actions, hidden modes, alternate functions, or
patch-persisted non-control state. In particular, there is no seed parameter.

### Inputs

| Port | Label/signal | Declared voltage | Normative behavior |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `rateCV` | `Rate`, `cv` | `-5..+5 V`, normal `0 V` | Exponential modulation at `1 V/oct`: multiply knob speed by `2^clamp(rateCV, -5, 5)`, then clamp final speed to `0.000625..20` model units/s. Negative CV extends the slow end to Sloth-like times; positive CV cannot exceed the modulation-rate ceiling. |
| `characterCV` | `Char`, `cv` | `-5..+5 V`, normal `0 V` | `effectiveCharacter = clamp(character + characterCV / 5, 0, 1)`. Thus either rail can sweep/clamp the full Character domain. |
| `reset` | `Reset`, `trigger` | `0..10 V`, normal `0 V` | A rising edge at `>=1 V` restores the exact reset state once; a held-high signal does not repeatedly reset. It must fall below `1 V` before another edge is accepted. |

Input arrays are allocated once, initialized to their declared `0 V` normals,
and never replaced. There are no input normalizations between jacks.

### Outputs and LEDs

| Port/state | Signal/range | Normative behavior |
|---|---:|---|
| `x` | `cv`, `-5..+5 V` | Conditioned Lorenz X coordinate. |
| `y` | `cv`, `-5..+5 V` | Conditioned Lorenz Y coordinate; correlated with X but not a phase-shifted or scaled copy. |
| `z` | `cv`, `-5..+5 V` | Lorenz Z coordinate recentered around the current non-zero-equilibrium height. |
| `lobe` | `gate`, `0/10 V` | `10 V` while internal `x >= 0`, otherwise `0 V`. It indicates the currently occupied wing; it is not a periodic clock or random pulse train. |
| `xLed`, `yLed`, `zLed` | LED `0..1` | Final sample of the corresponding output mapped as `clamp(volts / 10 + 0.5, 0, 1)`; `0 V` is `0.5`. |
| `lobeLed` | LED `0/1` | Mirrors the final Lobe gate state. |

Output buffers are allocated once and completely filled in place on every
`process()` call. X, Y, and Z are DC-coupled modulation CV, not pitch outputs.

## Voltage, Timing, and Reset Contract

- X/Y/Z are always finite and bounded to the project's bipolar CV/audio rails,
  `-5..+5 V`. Depth zero produces exactly `0 V` on all three axes.
- Lobe uses the project gate standard, exactly `0 V` or `10 V`. Orbit 3's
  equilibrium output is `0/5 V`; the doubled high level is an explicit project
  adaptation.
- Reset uses the project's `1 V` trigger threshold. Orbit 3 documents a
  Schmitt-style `+2 V` low/`+3 V` high threshold; that hardware threshold is not
  copied because this app consistently accepts triggers at `>=1 V`.
- There is no clock input, trigger output, audio input, or 1 V/oct pitch input.
- Rate is not a promised cycle frequency: a chaotic trajectory has variable
  lobe residence. With default state and Character, the first positive-to-
  negative lobe crossing occurs after about `1.1117` model-time units. This is
  about `1.76 s` at default Rate, `55.6 s` at the knob minimum, and `29.6 min`
  with Rate at minimum plus `-5 V` CV. Later dwell times are intentionally
  unequal.
- `reset()` (the lifecycle method) restores the same internal state and reset
  edge detector, fills all three stable input arrays with their declared `0 V`
  normals in place, clears every output buffer to `0`, sets axis LEDs to `0.5`,
  and sets `lobeLed` to `0`. It never replaces input or output identities.
- A Reset input edge is sample-accurate: restore state before the RK4 advance for
  that sample, then emit the newly advanced coordinates and Lobe state.

## Normative DSP Model

### Continuous system

Use the conventional three-state Lorenz equations from Lorenz (1963), also used
by Mutable Instruments' Streams implementation:

```text
dx/dt = sigma * (y - x)
dy/dt = x * (rho - z) - y
dz/dt = x * y - beta * z

sigma = 10
beta  = 8/3
rho   = 24 + 12 * effectiveCharacter
```

At the default `rho = 28`, these are the classic chaotic parameters. No noise,
random draw, dither, or hidden perturbation may be added.

### Initial/reset state

Use this exact state for construction, lifecycle reset, Reset input edges, and
numerical recovery:

```text
x = 13.79322908927515
y = 12.951847112857063
z = 34.901636990308354
```

This is a point on the established classic trajectory, obtained offline by
starting at `(1, 1, 1)` and applying 20,000 classical RK4 steps of `0.001` at
`rho=28`. Hardcoding the three scalars avoids a long startup transient and avoids
performing a burn-in loop on the audio thread. It is a software adaptation, not
a measured hardware power-on state.

### Integration

For each output sample:

1. Sanitize parameter and input values. Non-finite controls fall back to their
   documented defaults; non-finite input samples become `0 V`.
2. Detect Reset's rising edge and, if present, restore the exact reset state.
3. Compute the sample's effective speed and `rho` using the mappings above.
4. Set `dt = effectiveSpeed / sampleRate` and advance X/Y/Z by one classical
   fourth-order Runge–Kutta step. Hold `rho` constant across the four RK stages
   for that sample.
5. If any state is non-finite or `abs(state) > 1000`, restore the reset state for
   deterministic recovery before output conditioning.
6. Condition the coordinates, write the four outputs, and continue.

RK4 is chosen over Streams' fixed-point forward Euler update. At the supported
minimum production sample rate and maximum speed, `dt <= 20 / 44100`, leaving a
large numerical margin; RK4 also makes short-horizon behavior effectively
sample-rate invariant. Adaptive integration is rejected because variable work
and rejected steps are poor fits for a real-time worklet.

### Output conditioning

Use the existing `softLimitVoltage(value, 5, 0.9)` utility. For the current
sample's state and `rho`:

```text
xRaw = 0.25 * x
yRaw = 0.20 * y
zRaw = 0.20 * (z - (rho - 1))

xOut = depth * softLimitVoltage(xRaw, 5, 0.9)
yOut = depth * softLimitVoltage(yRaw, 5, 0.9)
zOut = depth * softLimitVoltage(zRaw, 5, 0.9)
lobe = x >= 0 ? 10 : 0
```

The scaling places the classic attractor usefully across the bipolar CV range.
Recentering Z around `rho - 1` follows the height of the Lorenz system's two
non-zero equilibria. Soft limiting is output-only: it guarantees rails without
flattening the internal attractor state. It is an emulator voltage policy, not
an assertion that either reference module saturates this way.

## Worklet Plan and Trade-offs

- Allocate three input and four output `Float32Array`s only in `createDSP()`.
- Keep X/Y/Z and edge history as scalar closure state. Write RK4 with scalar
  temporaries; do not create arrays, objects, closures, or event payloads in the
  sample loop.
- Keep CV response sample-rate and sample-accurate. Knob defaults may be
  sanitized once per block, but Rate CV, Character CV, and Reset must be read per
  sample.
- Do not use DOM, `window`, `document`, timers, main-thread Web Audio nodes,
  `Math.random`, services, telemetry, or module events.
- Classical RK4 costs four derivative evaluations per sample, more than Euler or
  midpoint integration. The state is only three-dimensional and capped below
  audio rate, so the quality/sample-rate consistency trade-off is appropriate;
  `audit:dsp --matrix` must still measure it.
- The conditioner calls the existing exponential soft limiter on up to three
  samples per frame. If profiling later shows a problem, an alternative must be
  researched and must preserve the exact rail and monotonicity contract; silent
  substitution with hard clipping is not allowed.
- Floating-point chaos is practically aperiodic, not mathematically guaranteed
  never to repeat forever. Two engines may eventually diverge after tiny
  numerical differences. Reset guarantees exact replay only for the same
  implementation, sample rate, parameter/CV sequence, and processing order.

## Contradictions, Decisions, and Assumptions

| Evidence or uncertainty | Decision |
|---|---|
| The Triple Sloth guide first says X/Y/Z differ, then says Z is simply inverted Y. | Treat this as a hardware/source wording contradiction. The adaptation exposes the three distinct Lorenz coordinates and does not copy Sloth's inversion. |
| The single-Sloth product page says its pot changes balance, not frequency; its build guide says the pot alters frequency a little. | Separate the concepts: Rate changes only time scaling; Character changes `rho` and therefore orbit geometry/residence. Minor analogue cross-coupling is not emulated. |
| Sloth evidence ranges from mostly ±2 V/roughly ±3 V to a “big” output near ±5 V; Orbit 3 states 3–12 Vpp depending on settings. | Translate all axes to a declared, conditioned `±5 V` project contract with a Depth control. |
| Orbit 3 is a modified analogue double-scroll system and Sloth's exact transfer equations are not published in the panel manual. | Implement the fully documented Lorenz equations as an inspired software model; do not claim hardware fidelity. |
| Hardware parameter modulation may produce jumps, periodic windows, stable motion, or visually large but sonically subtle changes. | Character is constrained to `rho=24..36`, defaults to the verified classic value 28, and may legitimately visit ordered behavior. Tests require finite bounded state, not “maximum chaos” at every setting. |
| “Never repeating” is practical hardware language, while finite floating point has a finite state space. | Describe the result as deterministic, sensitive, and practically aperiodic. Do not promise an infinite mathematical period. |
| There is no hardware-derived reset state or exact software output scaling. | Use the documented offline burn-in point and explicit conditioner above; both are normative emulator assumptions. |
| A logistic map is named in the queue's research targets. | Reject it for v1 because a discrete map would require clocked/stepped behavior already served by `rnd`, `sh`, and `turing`. Reconsider only as a separately justified module. |

There is no unresolved contradiction blocking implementation. The audible feel
and the usefulness of the `rho=24..36` panel mapping still need listening review
after implementation, but the range is bounded, defaults to the primary classic
parameter set, and has exact acceptance behavior.

### Golden default trajectory

At `sampleRate=48000`, `bufferSize=128`, default parameters, and zero inputs,
the output (after Float32 storage) must agree within `1e-6 V` with:

| Sample | X | Y | Z | Lobe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `0` | `3.4482796192` | `2.5900843143` | `1.5805529356` | `10` |
| `127` | `3.4444050789` | `2.5536072254` | `1.6086698771` | `10` |

The final axis LEDs must equal the final stored voltages divided by 10 plus
0.5 (within `1e-7`), and `lobeLed` must be `1`.

After exactly one real second at 48 kHz with the same settings, the final X/Y/Z
must be within `1e-5 V` of `(2.454939, 3.208325, -1.696188)`. Repeating for one
second at 44.1 and 96 kHz must remain within `1e-5 V` per axis of the 48 kHz
result. This is a short-horizon integration check, not a promise that chaotic
paths stay aligned indefinitely across sample rates.

### Controls and CV

5. Over one second at 48 kHz, Rate `0` has total three-axis sample-to-sample
   variation between `0.7` and `1.0 V`; Rate `1` has variation greater than
   `500 V` and more than 100 times the Rate-0 result. Rate must not change the
   reset point or conditioner scaling.
6. With default Rate, constant `+1 V` Rate CV doubles effective model speed to
   `1.2649110641` units/s. After one second its final axes must be within
   `1e-5 V` of `(-0.143663, -0.208829, -3.078136)`, with one Lobe transition
   for the specified reset state. `-5 V` and `+5 V` exercise the documented
   speed clamps; all outputs stay finite and in range.
7. Character `0`, `1/3`, and `1` map exactly to `rho=24`, `28`, and `36`.
   Character CV `+5 V` at default must be sample-for-sample identical to
   Character `1` with zero CV; `-5 V` must match Character `0`.
8. After one second at default Rate, Character `0` and Character `1` final axes
   are respectively within `1e-5 V` of
   `(-1.959004, -2.504195, -1.764815)` and
   `(3.167364, 2.244047, 1.138022)`. This confirms Character changes the
   trajectory rather than merely scaling an output.
9. Two reset-matched instances at Depth `1` and `0.5` produce identical Lobe
   gates and the second instance's X/Y/Z are exactly half the first within
   Float32 tolerance. Depth `0` makes X/Y/Z exactly zero and the axis LEDs
   exactly `0.5` while Lobe continues to evolve.

### Deterministic chaos, gate, and reset

10. Two instances with identical sample rate, controls, inputs, and reset timing
    are sample-for-sample equal. Processing must still work when `Math.random`
    is temporarily replaced by a throwing stub, demonstrating that no random
    source is consulted.
11. With Rate `1`, give one instance a `0.0001 V` Character CV perturbation for
    the first sample only, then feed both instances identical zeros. After one
    second the Euclidean distance between their X/Y/Z output vectors is greater
    than `1 V`; both remain bounded. This is the finite sensitivity target.
12. For Depth greater than zero, every non-zero X sample has `lobe=10 V` when X
    is positive and `lobe=0 V` when X is negative. Lobe contains only exact 0 or
    10 values and its LED mirrors the final sample.
13. Reset at `0.99 V` does not fire. A transition to `1 V` fires once; holding
    `10 V` does not fire again; falling below `1 V` then rising fires again. The
    first sample following each accepted edge matches a fresh instance under
    the same per-sample controls.
14. Lifecycle `reset()` preserves all buffer identities, clears outputs to zero,
    restores axis LEDs to `0.5` and `lobeLed` to `0`, clears edge history, and
    makes the next processed block reproduce the golden fresh block.

### Ranges, robustness, and audit

15. For every parameter extreme and `-5/0/+5 V` CV combination, X/Y/Z stay
    within `[-5, 5]`, Lobe stays in `{0,10}`, LEDs stay within `[0,1]`, and every
    sample is finite.
16. NaN/Infinity parameters and input samples use documented fallbacks and do
    not contaminate later samples. Every output sample is overwritten each
    block.
17. Input and output identities remain stable across process/reset and across
    the 44.1/48/96 kHz by 128/512-sample matrix.
18. Characterize two seconds at Rate `1`: sampled X/Y/Z block tuples must not
    repeat exactly and Lobe dwell lengths must not all be equal. This is a
    regression observation, not a proof of mathematical chaos.
19. Run the strict audit matrix and require zero voltage/finite/buffer-identity
    flags. Record timing because RK4 and three soft limiters are the main CPU
    risk.

## Source Quality Notes

- Manufacturer product pages and manuals are highest quality for their own
  panels, timings, thresholds, and stated voltage ranges. They describe two
  different analogue systems and cannot establish Lorenz transfer functions.
- Lorenz, May, and Chua are primary mathematical/historical sources. They do not
  select musical knob ranges or Eurorack voltage conditioning.
- Mutable Instruments and Ornament & Crime are the strongest comparable digital
  implementation references. Their update rates, fixed-point formats, output
  mappings, and host hardware differ, so this specification adopts the equations
  and interaction lessons rather than line-by-line code.
- The ADC presentation usefully summarizes musical design practice and warns to
  constrain parameter space, but it is secondary and non-peer-reviewed.
- Reviews and videos are appropriate evidence for observed workflow and sound;
  they do not override manufacturer electrical data or the normative contracts
  in this document.
